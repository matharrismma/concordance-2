# 01 — The World

## The short version

Superpowered people, **capes**, appeared around the world over roughly forty years. For most of that
time they were celebrities, soldiers, vigilantes and brands. Seven years ago, one night in
Rotterdam ended all of that. Now unsanctioned power use is a crime in 140 countries, and most heroes
are unemployed.

Then someone found a place where powers don't work.

## Key concepts

### Capes
In the world's language, *cape* means any superpowered person, whether or not they wear one. Most
do. A cape is part costume, part identity, and part a licence plate. Accord-badged capes wear
regulation colours, and "unregistered" capes wear whatever they want.

### The Aegis Accord
The establishment. It isn't cartoon-evil. It's the body that answered Glass Night by locking
everything down, and most of the world thanks it for that. It's bureaucratic, image-obsessed and
frightened. Its official position is that SIXFOLD is "an unlicensed spectacle exploiting
vulnerable former heroes," and it's working to shut the show down. Its real fear is the ninety
missing seconds of Glass Night.

### The Hush
The dampening field inside the Hex. Within it:
- powers don't fire, with no exceptions;
- capes are exactly as strong, fast and durable as their bodies are without powers (most of them
  are elite athletes, because they trained for decades);
- sound inside feels slightly muted. Wrestlers describe it as "being underwater without the water."

**The Hush ends exactly at the rim.** One step onto the floor outside and you're *loud* again. That
is why leaving the pit is a disqualification. In-world it's a legal line as much as a rule: if a
cape uses powers while fighting, SIXFOLD becomes an illegal power-fight broadcast, and the Accord
gets its excuse.

Nobody knows what generates the Hush. That is the central mystery of the series (see Season One,
and `03_ROSTER.md` → **NULL**).

### Going loud
To *go loud* is to leave the Hush, or to use a power anywhere near the pit. In the Hex it means an
instant DQ. On the street, the phrase has become slang for losing control.

### Why heroes wrestle
- **Money.** Grounded capes have no income. SIXFOLD pays, and pays well.
- **Legitimacy.** The Hex is the only place a cape can fight in public legally.
- **Truth.** In the Hush, nobody's power carries them. Who you are underneath is all that's left.
  For heroes who spent careers being invincible, that's terrifying and addictive.
- **Grudges.** Forty years of hero politics, betrayals, rivalries and Glass Night itself. The Hex is
  where those get settled.

## The fighting-game frame

SIXFOLD is built like a fighting-game universe:

- **A global roster** with distinct nations, silhouettes and styles, each one recognisable from a
  single still frame.
- **A mysterious organiser.** **VALE**, the masked host of SIXFOLD LIVE, who found the Hush and
  built the show around it. Formerly the hero **Lodestar**, presumed dead at Glass Night.
- **An annual tournament.** *The Sixfold*: six cities, one Crown (see `04`).
- **Per-character story modes.** Every roster member has a personal arc that can run as a web
  series, a set of vlogs or an angle (see `03`).
- **A hidden final boss.** Whoever, or whatever, is under the Hex.

## Tone

- **Grounded superhero.** Think a prestige TV hero drama crossed with combat-sports documentary
  style. Powers are rumour, consequence and memory, almost never spectacle.
- **Earnest athletics.** The wrestling is played completely straight. Pain is real, and the Hush
  makes everyone mortal.
- **Stream-native chaos.** The production is a character too: things break, chat reacts, and the
  host improvises.
- **Hope with a bruise on it.** Heroes who lost everything get to be heroic again, one match at a
  time.
