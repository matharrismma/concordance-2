# 02 — The Hex

## The pit

The Hex is a **sunken, canvas-floored hexagon** ringed by six padded walls instead of ropes. The
crowd sits on risers and looks *down* into it, like a cockfight pit, an operating theatre or an
old bear-baiting ring. There are no ropes, no turnbuckles and no apron. The geometry gives the
promotion its whole look and its whole rulebook.

```
                 POST 1 (The Crown Post, hard cam side)
                    _________________
         POST 6   /                   \   POST 2
                 /                     \
                /        THE HEX        \
               |        (canvas)         |
  THE LEDGE -> |                         |
 (tag alcove)   \                       /
         POST 5  \_____________________/  POST 3
                        POST 4
                   (Entrance stair)
   ---- the RIM: top edge of the six walls; the Hush ends at its outer edge ----
   ---- the FLOOR: anything outside the rim. Touch it and you're loud. ----
```

### Key parts

| Part | What it is | Rule significance |
|---|---|---|
| **The Canvas** | The fighting surface: a sprung deck under canvas, like a ring. | Inside the Hush. |
| **The Walls** | Six padded walls, roughly chest height from the canvas. | Wrestlers rebound off them in place of ropes. Inside the Hush. |
| **The Rim** | The flat, padded top of each wall. | **Still inside.** You can stand, fight and dive from the rim. |
| **The Posts** | Six vertical posts at the vertices. | Capes are hung here before the bell. Climbing a post is legal, but a high-risk spot. |
| **The Floor** | Everything outside the rim. | **Touch it = DQ.** |
| **The Ledge** | A recessed alcove cut into one wall, still inside the Hush. | Where tag partners wait. |
| **The Stair** | A removable staircase at Post 4 for entrances. | Pulled away at the bell. |

## The rules of the Hex

### How you win
1. **Pinfall.** Both shoulders down for a three count by the Warden.
2. **Submission.** A tap, or a verbal quit. In the Hex it's called *asking for quiet*.
3. **Count-out (Down Count).** If a wrestler can't stand by a ten count, the match ends. With no
   ropes and no floor to escape to, the down count *replaces* the ring-out count.
4. **Disqualification.** See below.

### How you lose by DQ

**The Exit Rule:** if any part of a wrestler's body touches **the Floor** outside the rim, that
wrestler is **disqualified**, immediately and without appeal. **The Hex doesn't care how you left.**

Thrown, suplexed over, dived, slipped: you lose. This is the heart of SIXFOLD storytelling.

- **The rim is in.** A wrestler fighting on top of the wall is still alive.
- **Hanging is in.** A wrestler dangling over the outside of the wall, holding on without touching
  the floor, is still alive. These are SIXFOLD's signature near-falls, the **teeter**.
- **Both out, both lose.** If both wrestlers touch the floor, it's a **double DQ**, called *a loud
  finish*. Crowds hate it, and heels love it.
- **Simultaneous isn't a thing.** The rim judges call who touched first, frame by frame on replay
  if they have to. Replays become content (see `06`).

Other DQ offences:
- **Going loud.** Any use of powers, by a wrestler or by interference from outside the pit.
  Since powers are never shown live, this is played through production cues (lights crackling,
  feed glitches, the Warden's tablet reading the field) and the aftermath.
- **Weapons from the floor.** Anything that wasn't in the pit at the bell. Some match types change
  this.
- **Striking a Warden.** Standard.
- **Touching the cape.** Pulling an opponent's cape off a post during the match, as an insult or a
  weapon, is a DQ. It's also the single worst sign of disrespect in the Hex, and it's how feuds
  start.

### Exit-Rule strategy (why this changes wrestling)

Because getting thrown out means losing, a **ring-out is a finish**:
- Powerhouses win by **throwing**: a bodyslam over the rim is a match-ending move.
- Smaller wrestlers win by **surviving**: rim-walking, teetering and reversing on the edge.
- Every high-flying spot has stakes. A dive *off* the rim *into* the pit is legal. Overshoot it and
  you lose.
- Heels **bait** opponents toward the edge. Faces **fight back from the brink**.
- The audience learns to watch **feet**. Chat erupts every time a heel touches the rim.

## The officials

| Role | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **The Warden** | The referee inside the pit, in grey with a hexagonal badge. | Counts, calls submissions, enforces the rules, carries the field tablet. |
| **Rim Judges (×2)** | Seated at opposite vertices, sumo-style, on raised stools. | Watch *only* for floor contact. Raise a red flag on a DQ. Can call for replay. |
| **The Keeper of Posts** | A ceremonial role. | Takes each cape at the rim and hangs it on that wrestler's post. Returns it at the end of the match, or to the winner in cape-stakes matches. |

The Warden's tablet shows a live **Hush reading**. When it spikes, someone's trying to go loud. It
works as an in-world device for building tension, and as a camera insert.

## The cape ritual

Every entrance ends the same way. The wrestler reaches the rim at **Post 4**, unclasps their cape,
and hands it to the Keeper of Posts, who hangs it on the wrestler's assigned post. The wrestler
steps down into the pit, and the Stair is pulled away.

- **Capes off in the Hex.** No exceptions. It also protects the wrestlers, since nobody can get
  tangled.
- A hero who **refuses** to remove their cape forfeits. This has happened once (see Season One).
- In **Cape vs. Cape** matches, the winner takes the loser's cape. The loser can never wear that
  identity again. It's SIXFOLD's equivalent of mask vs. mask, and the most sacred stipulation in the
  company.

## Match types

| Match | Rules |
|---|---|
| **Standard Hex** | All the rules above. |
| **Tag (Ledge Rules)** | Two-on-two. The legal partner fights, the other waits in the Ledge. Tags happen at the Ledge mouth. A partner who leaves the Ledge to brawl is legal only on the canvas, and still loses for the team if they touch the floor. |
| **Six-Post** | Six wrestlers, one per post. Eliminations by pin, submission or exit. Last one in wins. |
| **Last Light** | A battle royal. Entrants come in at intervals, exits only, last one in the pit wins. The annual opening event of the Sixfold tournament. |
| **Brink** | Pinfalls and submissions don't count. The only way to win is to put your opponent on the floor. |
| **No Rim** | The walls are lowered to knee height. Survival is nearly impossible, so matches are short and violent. |
| **Cape vs. Cape** | Standard rules. The loser forfeits their cape and identity forever. |
| **Silent Match** | The crowd is asked to stay silent, the Booth goes quiet, and the stream carries only raw pit audio. A gimmick for blood feuds. |
| **Loud Rules** | *Doesn't exist.* It's a rumoured "no-Hush" match that the Accord keeps claiming SIXFOLD is planning. It's a myth used for storytelling and should never actually happen (see Season One finale). |

## Physical build spec (real-world production)

The Hex has to be safe to perform on, and the Exit Rule means people *will* go over the walls on
purpose. Treat the following as a starting brief for a ring builder and a safety officer. It isn't
a final spec.

### Deck
- Hexagon roughly **6.1 m (20 ft) across, flat to flat**. That's close to a standard 20 ft ring, so
  wrestlers' timing and spacing carry over.
- Standard ring construction: steel or wood frame, sprung boards, foam underlay, canvas cover.
- The "sunken" look comes from the **risers being built up around it**. The deck sits at normal ring
  height and the audience floor is raised. The pit is a sightline effect, not a hole in the ground,
  which keeps under-deck access for crew.

### Walls
- About **1.1–1.2 m (42–48 in) above the canvas**. That's high enough to read as a pit and low
  enough to be climbed and teetered on.
- **Built to flex like ropes.** A spring-mounted frame with thick high-density foam and a vinyl skin,
  so wrestlers can rebound off them. *This is the main engineering problem, so prototype it before
  committing to the format.*
- **Rim cap** about 30 cm (12 in) wide, padded, with a non-slip top so wrestlers can stand on it.

### The floor (the DQ zone)
- A crash zone at least **2.5 m wide around the full perimeter**, at canvas level or lower,
  covered in crash mats like a gymnastics landing area, with no hard floor showing.
- No barricade, crowd or equipment inside the crash zone. The risers start behind it.
- Rim judges' stools sit *outside* the crash zone or on platforms built into the riser face.

### Stunts
- Every planned exit is **rehearsed on the crash zone** with a spotter.
- Teeters, where a wrestler hangs on the outside of the wall, need a dedicated toe-ledge or hidden
  grip built into the outer wall face.
- A safety officer has authority to stop any match. The Warden carries an "X" signal that reads in
  kayfabe as a field emergency.
