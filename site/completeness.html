Completeness — the hole in the table is the prediction

Some fields have a closed structure: a few fundamental quantities whose pairwise relations form a complete table. When one cell is empty, symmetry does not merely suggest — it demands that something fill it. This is the Atlas as a predictor, not only a map: the same move that placed every calculation, at the grade of a fundamental element.

The four circuit variables — Chua's square

Four circuit variables — voltage v, current i, charge q, and magnetic flux φ — admit six pairwise relations. Two are definitions (q is the integral of i; φ is the integral of v), three are the classic passive elements (resistor, capacitor, inductor), and the sixth — linking flux to charge — was EMPTY. Chua argued in 1971 that an element must fill it. HP built the memristor in 2008. The hole in the table was the prediction.

The two definitions and the three known elements filled five edges. The sixth — the flux–charge diagonal, M — was empty. That emptiness was the prediction.
