# Recognized Scripture chiasms — a gathered catalog to feed the assay

Matt, 2026-08-26: *"Do research to see any other chiasms that have been recognized in scripture."*

This is a GATHERING, not a claim (the covenant: gather and attribute, never author). Each structure
below is a chiasm proposed by named scholarship — a *tune* that says where to look. Our job is the
assay: measure whether the proposed mirror pairs echo above a null (as we did for Revelation, p=0, and
the Sermon on the Mount). The measure then sorts a **lexically-woven** chiasm (repeated vocabulary at
mirror points — measurable, like Revelation) from a **thematic** one (mirror in idea, not words — not
lexically confirmable, like the SoM's global frame). Both are honest outcomes.

**Prior art for the method.** Computational chiasm detection is an active field — e.g. *Computational
Discovery of Chiasmus in Ancient Religious Text* (arXiv 2501.10739, 2025). Our contribution is the
null-tested lexical assay wired to the fascia measure; the catalog is the roadmap of targets.

---

## Already measured (this project)
- **Revelation 1–22** — whole-book macro-chiasm, pivot **12:9–10** (Satan cast down // the Kingdom of
  Christ). Matt's diagram. **CONFIRMED, p=0.0000** (§13). A *global lexical* chiasm.
- **Sermon on the Mount, Matthew 5:3–7:27** — concentric about the Lord's Prayer. Global mirror **not**
  lexically confirmed (COINCIDENCE); real **local frames** measured instead — Beatitudes inclusio
  (5:3↔5:10), "Law and the Prophets" bracket (5:17↔7:12), the secret-reward triad (6:4/6:6/6:18) (§14).

## Whole-book / whole-narrative chiasms (best next targets)
- **Genesis Flood, 6:9–9:29** — centre **8:1 "God remembered Noah"**. Wenham (1978). The textbook
  chiasm: mirror pairs carry repeated NUMBERS and vocabulary — 7 days (7:10↔8:12), 40 days
  (7:12↔8:6), 150 days rise/fall (7:24↔8:3), enter/leave the ark (7:1–5↔8:15–19), covenant
  (6:18↔9:9). **Highly measurable** — the strongest next assay.
- **Tower of Babel, 11:1–9** — a tight palistrophe centred on **v5, the LORD came down**. Fokkelman;
  Wenham. "one language / whole earth" (v1) ↔ "confused / scattered over the earth" (v9); "come, let
  us make bricks" ↔ "come, let us confuse". Short, dense, lexical — an excellent measurable target.
- **Jonah** — two mirrored halves (ch 1–2 // ch 3–4); Jonah's psalm (ch 2) is itself chiastic, focus
  "Salvation belongs to the LORD" (2:9). Wendland's "what makes a chiasm valid" test case.
- **Ruth** — book-long chiasm, turning on Naomi's reversal (the "empty → full" arc; 1:21 ↔ 4:14–15).
- **Leviticus** — the whole book centred on **ch 16, the Day of Atonement** (Aaron entering the Most
  Holy Place). Large, ritual-structured; likely more thematic than lexical.
- **Amos** — the oracles against the nations (1–2) plus a book-long frame.
- **Mark** — intercalations ("Markan sandwiches", A-B-A') and a proposed book-long structure.
- **John** — the seven signs form a chiasm centred on Jesus as giver of life; Raymond Brown identified
  chiasms in 6:36–40, 15:7–17, 16:16–31, 18:28–19:16a, 19:16b–42.
- **Hebrews** — proposed book-long chiasm centred on the new covenant / high-priestly core (chs 8–9).

## Passage / pericope chiasms (measurable, high value)
- **John's Prologue, 1:1–18** — concentric, centred on **1:12–13** (becoming children of God). Widely
  analysed (Boismard, Culpepper). Tight and lexical (Word/God/light/life mirror) — strong target.
- **Colossians 1:15–20 — the Christ hymn** — chiastic, centred on the supremacy of Christ. **This is
  the fascia's own ground text: 1:17 "in him all things hold together."** Measuring it is
  self-referential — the passage about all things cohering is itself a chiasm cohering about Christ.
  Priority.
- **Philippians 2:6–11 — the Christ hymn** — descent/ascent V-shape (kenosis → exaltation), centred on
  the cross (2:8). Concentric, well-attested.
- **1 Corinthians 13** — chiastic on love (13:1–3 / 13:8–13 frame the central 13:4–7).
- **Psalm 23** — concentric, centred on **"for you are with me"** (23:4).
- **Zechariah 1–6 — the night visions** — eight visions in a chiasm centred on the fourth/fifth (the
  lampstand and olive trees / the flying scroll).
- **2 Samuel 21–24 — the "Samuel appendix"** — a famous six-part chiasm (two narratives, two lists,
  two poems) framing David's reign.
- **Genesis 9:6** — a verse-level ABCC'B'A' ("whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall his blood
  be shed"). A minimal, exactly-lexical chiasm — a clean unit test for the measure.

## Recommended assay order (measurability × attestation × mission)
1. **Genesis Flood (6:9–9:29, Wenham)** — clearest, most lexical, numeric mirrors. Do first.
2. **Colossians 1:15–20** — the fascia's ground text; self-referential; short and lexical.
3. **Tower of Babel (11:1–9)** — tight, dense, lexical; small enough to pair verse-by-verse.
4. **John's Prologue (1:1–18)** and **Philippians 2:6–11** — well-attested passage chiasms.
5. **Jonah** and **Ruth** — book-long; test the whole-book measure again on a different genre.

Each target, measured the same way: pull the proposed mirror units, echo = Jaccard of content stems,
null = permute the pairing / shuffle order. Report per-pair echoes and the aggregate vs the null;
CONFIRM the lexical chiasms, and honestly name the thematic ones the lexical measure cannot see.
